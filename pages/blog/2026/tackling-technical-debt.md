# Tackling Technical Debt: Building a Culture of Collaboration and Refactoring

Technical debt is a real pain point for many software development teams. It's like a pile of shortcuts and compromises that might help us get the job done now, but they'll slow us down in the long run. To fix it, we can't just rely on our coding skills. We need to change our culture, communicate better, and keep improving.

Here's how I tackled technical debt by creating a culture of refactoring, building strong relationships with product management, and being open and honest.

## 1. Cultivating a Culture of Refactoring

Refactoring became the cornerstone of our approach to managing technical debt. To make refactoring a valued part of our process, I did a few things:

- **Made refactoring part of our delivery plan.** Refactoring wasn't seen as an extra task or something we could skip. It was a natural part of building quality software.
- **Celebrated our wins.** Whenever refactoring made our code better or sped up our development, we made sure to shout it from the rooftops. This showed the team that investing in clean code was worth it.
- **Gave our team the power to make changes.** I encouraged developers to find opportunities for refactoring during our refinement sessions and let them own the process of improvement.

## 2. Building Strong Relationships with Product Management

Working with Product Managers (PMs) was super important. Technical debt often comes from the intersection of technical and business goals, so we needed to make sure both sides were on the same page:

- **Talked things through.** I made sure to explain the challenges of delivering features in a system that's loaded with technical debt, in non-technical terms, so PMs understood the business impact.
- **Fostered trust and collaboration.** We made sure tackling technical debt wasn't seen as a battle between delivering features and fixing bugs. Instead, we saw it as a way to help our team achieve our long-term goals.
- **Coached across disciplines.** I made sure Product Managers and developers understood each other's roles. This helped us work together more effectively and make better decisions.
- **Refined team backlog sessions.** Every two weeks, when we started a new iteration, the team would come together to work on the backlog. These sessions were super important for figuring out which technical debt to tackle first.
- **Led through transparency and coaching.** I believed in empowering the team and stakeholders with knowledge. I explained complex things in a way that everyone could understand, and helped developers and PMs understand each other's perspectives.

## The Result

We were able to reduce technical debt and build a stronger, more collaborative team.

A learning culture took hold: team members grew in their understanding of both technical and business domains, making the entire team more adaptable and effective.

Addressing technical debt is not a one-time project but an ongoing journey. By creating a culture of refactoring, encouraging collaboration, and investing in transparency and coaching, we transformed how the team viewed and managed technical debt. These strategies didn't just tackle the debt itself - they laid the groundwork for a stronger, more aligned team.
